# Copy review: MERCER VALE

8 November 2026. The temporary Russian version has been replaced with an English editorial rewrite, rather than a literal back-translation. The Russian files are preserved in output/russian-backup. Existing type families, photography, layout and core interactions remain.

## Diagnosis and voice

Situation A: the earlier copy repeated broad ideas about life, intention, familiar shapes and personal rhythm. Most headings could be moved to an unrelated clothing brand unchanged. Product evidence is limited: this is a first collection in development, represented by three clothing studies and an opening reference photograph.

The writing voice is a London clothing-house editor talking through the references on the page. It can describe proportion, colour and combinations precisely. It cannot credibly supply a manufacturing history, fabric composition, customer experience or environmental certification.

Use one expressive headline, then let the clothes supply the details. Prefer British English, concrete garment names, short factual interface labels and contractions in prose. Keep a longer explanation in About, with shorter image captions elsewhere. Avoid matching every section to the same slogan/two-sentence pattern.

### Headline candidates considered

- **Take the long way home.** Chosen: a visible action and a natural continuation of the established campaign idea.
- **Leave the house. Stay out.** Rejected: forceful, but potentially reads as exclusion and uses a mannered pair of imperatives.
- **Clothes for the days you don’t plan.** Rejected: returns to a broad lifestyle promise without a Mercer Vale-specific fact.

The chosen headline is campaign language, not a claim of a proven commercial differentiator. A stronger positioning claim would need a founder’s stated point of view and actual garment details.

## Editing pass

The final copy removes repeated life/rhythm slogans, abstract claims about being oneself, unsupported longevity language and duplicated section-ending lines. It uses varied paragraph lengths, recognisable items in the images, plain CTAs and British spellings such as colour and enquire. FAQ negatives state real availability limits; they are not rhetorical “not X, but Y” reveals. Image credits and development status remain visible where they affect the visitor’s understanding.

## Evidence still needed for product claims

- Fabric composition, weight, construction and garment measurements: a product specification sheet.
- Production locations, factory practices or certification claims: supplier records and applicable certificates.
- Founder history or brand differentiation beyond the current visual direction: a founder interview or approved positioning brief.
- Delivery, returns, launch dates or stock claims: confirmed commercial policies and inventory data.

None of these missing facts has been filled with invented copy.
